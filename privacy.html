MailDesk Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 9, 2026

MailDesk is an email client for Windows. It runs on your PC and connects from there directly to the mail providers of the accounts you add. There is no MailDesk account and no MailDesk server: the publisher of MailDesk does not receive your mail, your passwords, or any information about how you use the app.

In short

  • Your mail and your sign-in details stay on your PC and with your mail provider.
  • MailDesk contains no advertising, no analytics, and no crash reporting.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with anyone.

What MailDesk accesses

MailDesk accesses a mailbox only after you add the account. To work as a mail client it then reads and changes, in that mailbox, your messages and their attachments, your folders, and the state of messages (read, starred, moved, deleted), and it sends the messages you write. The email address and the name you enter identify the account in the app and appear as the sender of your messages.

How you sign in

  • Gmail and Google Workspace. You enter an app password that you create in your Google Account. MailDesk uses it to connect to Google's mail servers over IMAP and SMTP. MailDesk does not use Google Sign-In or the Gmail API.
  • Outlook.com and Hotmail. You sign in to Microsoft in your web browser. MailDesk never sees your Microsoft password. Microsoft gives MailDesk access tokens with which it reads your mailbox over IMAP and sends mail through Microsoft Graph. You can withdraw this access at any time in your Microsoft account, under Apps and services you've given access.
  • Other providers. You enter the password or app password of the mailbox. MailDesk uses it to connect to your provider over IMAP and SMTP.

What is stored on your PC

Everything MailDesk keeps is kept in your Windows user profile:

  • Sign-in details (passwords, app passwords, and Microsoft access tokens), encrypted with Windows data protection for your Windows user account.
  • A copy of recent mail (senders, recipients, subjects, and message text), so that folders open quickly and can be read without a connection. This copy is an ordinary file that is protected by your Windows account and is not encrypted separately. Mail from folders that you have not opened for 30 days is removed from it.
  • Attachments you save, in the folder Downloads\MailDesk.
  • Your settings, and a diagnostic log of technical events such as connection errors. The log can contain your email address, folder names, and, when a message cannot be processed, a fragment of that message. Passwords are not written to it, and it does not leave your PC unless you send it to someone yourself.

Who MailDesk connects to

  • The mail servers of the accounts you add.
  • Microsoft's sign-in service and Microsoft Graph, for Outlook.com and Hotmail accounts.
  • Server lookup. When you add an account at a provider MailDesk has no built-in settings for, and you do not enter the server settings yourself, MailDesk looks them up. Every lookup uses an encrypted connection (HTTPS). MailDesk first asks the autoconfiguration service of your mail domain, which receives your email address. Only if that gives no answer does it ask two public services, which receive nothing but the domain (the part after the @): Mozilla's database of mail providers and, if the domain is not listed there, Google Public DNS, to find the company that receives the domain's mail and look that company up in Mozilla's database. Your password is not part of any lookup. MailDesk shows you the servers it found, and sends your password to them only when you then choose to connect. Turn on manual server settings to avoid the lookup.
  • Pictures in messages. Messages can contain pictures that are loaded from the sender's server. Loading them tells that server when the message was opened and from which IP address. You can switch this off under Settings, Privacy, "Load pictures from the internet", and still show the pictures of a single message.
  • Links that you click open in your web browser, not in MailDesk.

Connections to Gmail, Microsoft, Yahoo, and iCloud are always encrypted. For other providers, MailDesk accepts servers from a lookup only if they use encryption (SSL/TLS or STARTTLS); in the manual server settings you choose the encryption yourself. An account that was added with MailDesk 1.0 keeps the settings it was added with, which for a few providers encrypt the connection only if the server offers it.

Notifications and verification codes

To announce new mail, MailDesk reads the sender, the subject, and the first lines of new messages on your PC. It also recognizes verification codes there, so that you can copy one with a click; copying places the code on the Windows clipboard. None of this leaves your PC. You can limit notifications to verification codes or turn them off in Settings.

What MailDesk does not do

  • It does not send your mail or your contacts to the publisher or to any third party.
  • It does not show advertising or build a profile of you.
  • It does not collect usage statistics or crash reports.
  • It does not use your data to train AI models.

Removing your data

  • Removing an account in MailDesk deletes its sign-in details and its copy of your mail from your PC.
  • Uninstalling the Microsoft Store version of MailDesk removes all data the app stored. Files you saved to Downloads\MailDesk are yours and stay.
  • Your mail itself is kept by your mail provider. Deleting MailDesk's data does not delete anything there.

Changes to this policy

When MailDesk changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy is updated on this page together with the date at the top.
